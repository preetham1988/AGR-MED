Diabetes and blood pressure

Long-term conditions, followed over time.

Diabetes and high blood pressure are managed best with regular, unhurried reviews. Readings, blood tests and medicines are looked at together, so treatment can be adjusted before problems build up.

What a review covers

  • Your sugar or blood pressure readings over time
  • Medicines, doses and side effects
  • Kidneys, heart, eyes and feet, checked at the right intervals
  • Practical advice on food, activity and sleep

Questions about general medicine.

Should I see a general physician or a specialist?

Start with a general consultation. Dr. Gangi Reddy TK is also a pulmonologist, so if your symptoms point to the lungs or to sleep, care continues with the same doctor.

How often should diabetes be reviewed?

Many people with diabetes are reviewed every three to six months, with an HbA1c blood test that shows average sugar control over about three months. Your doctor will suggest the right interval for you.

Do I need to fast before a blood test?

Some tests, such as fasting sugar and cholesterol, need 8 to 12 hours without food. Plain water is fine. The clinic will tell you when you book.

Can my eyes be checked for diabetes at the same clinic?

Yes. Diabetic eye screening is done at AGR Medical Center by the clinic’s eye specialist, Dr. Archana M.
